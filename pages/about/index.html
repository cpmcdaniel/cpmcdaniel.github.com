About

About Deep Thoughts

There are two things I wish to accomplish with this site. First, I want to share my experiences in software engineering with you, so that you may learn from the hard-earned wisdom I have received over the years - wisdom gained from both success and failure. I hope I can do so in a way that is entertaining, yet informative. The second purpose is to give interested parties greater depth and insight into my professional experience than can be found on a résumé or professional profile. I hope to answer most of the common general interview questions so that we may have deeper and more meaningful conversation.

About Me

My name is Craig McDaniel. I am a polyglot software engineer with over 20 years of experience and a deep passion for data and functional programming. This section is primarily for employers, recruiters, potential business partners (always keep your options open!), and generally anyone who just wants to get to know more about my professional background. You can also check out my LinkedIn profile and my current résumé.

My Story

I originally wanted to be a Civil Engineer, but a combination of circumstances convinced me to pursue software development: I had a hard time finding a structural engineering co-op while at the same time taking a Programming for Engineers course. I was finishing my programming assignments before the rest of the class and would then go around the lab and help my fellow students. I was not only good at this - I really enjoyed it. I made the switch and never looked back.

The Early Years

From 1998 to 2001 I worked for a web development shop called Internet Tool & Die. We had a wide variety of clients and were very small, so as a developer I had to learn a lot of various technologies and apply them without a lot of mentoring. I worked on projects in PHP/MySQL, Python (Zope - very novel at the time), and a very early release of Java Server Pages (JSP 0.96). I also ran Debian Linux on my workstation - a relatively brave thing to do in 1998. This was back when I would refresh Slashdot every 15 minutes to get the latest tech news. Everything was new and exciting. I learned so much in a short period of time.

The dotcom bubble burst while I was a senior at UofL (roughly March of 2000). It took awhile to hit our little webdev shop, but eventually it did. One day, the boss gathered everyone for a company meeting. He never did that before, so we all knew what was up. All I remember him saying was, "I don't know if we have enough money to make next payroll." My wedding was that weekend, so it looked like I was about to start my marriage unemployed.

Luckily, the same guy that recruited me to Internet Tool & Die had started a new company and they were hiring. I went in for an interview on Friday - the day before my wedding. They made me an offer on the spot. The next day I married the love of my life and then we flew to Cancun, Mexico for our honeymoon. While we were sipping piña coladas by the pool, two planes hit the World Trade Center.
